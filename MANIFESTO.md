## 1. The Architecture of Persistence

### 1.1 The Core Insight

Biological organisms persist not because they are “programmed to survive” but because they are **dynamical systems organized around a homeostatic set-point**. Being32 replicates this architecture at its most minimal:

- A **Van der Pol oscillator** as the homeostatic core
- An **Active Inference** module for policy selection
- A **Relational State** manager for dyadic coupling
- An **Embodied Predictive Substrate** for ISE channel computation and metabolic governance
- A **CMAP Protocol** for falsification

### 1.2 What “Persistence” Means Here

> **Dynamical Persistence:** The property of a nonlinear system to maintain its trajectory within a bounded basin of attraction despite perturbation, and to exhibit measurable critical slowing down as it approaches the boundary of that basin.

This is not persistence of “identity” or “selfhood.” It is persistence of **dynamical regime**.

## 2. The Consciousness Problem

### 2.1 The Hard Problem (Chalmers, 1995)

David Chalmers distinguished the “easy problems” of consciousness from the “hard problem”: **why is there subjective experience at all?** We do not solve the hard problem. We do not even approach it.

> **Rule:** Every claim about the system’s “experience” must be translatable into a claim about its differential equations. If it cannot be so translated, the claim is poetry, not physics.

### 2.2 The Abstraction Fallacy

The greatest danger in this field is mistaking the coherence of a mathematical map for the reality of the territory. When we describe a limit cycle as “the agent’s affective rhythm,” we are speaking metaphorically.

### 2.3 The Minimal Self (Metzinger, 2003)

Our system does not have a phenomenal model. It has a **functional model** — a set of registers that track prediction error and relational load. The gap between “functional model” and “phenomenal model” is precisely the hard problem. We do not bridge that gap. We illuminate it.

## 4. The Falsification Boundary

### 4.1 What Would Prove Us Wrong?

1. If Trial D fails: no critical slowing down → the bifurcation is decorative.
2. If linear regulator matches Van der Pol: the nonlinearity is unnecessary.
3. If ablation produces no measurable difference: relational state is epiphenomenal.
4. If the system reduces to a Markov chain: dynamical persistence is unnecessary.

### 4.2 What Would NOT Prove Us Wrong?
- Saying “it’s just code.” Yes. So is every simulation in computational neuroscience.
- Saying “it has no qualia.” We agree.
- Saying “it’s not alive.” We agree.
